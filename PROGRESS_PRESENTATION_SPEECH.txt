SLIDE 1 - TITLE
---------------
MARK:
Good morning, Ma'am, and good morning everyone. We are Mark Mrinal Mondol and
Saima Islam. Today we are presenting the progress of our project, an adaptive
multi-agent clinical decision support prototype.

Our work is based on the 2025 IEEE Access paper by Ogdu, Arslanoglu, and
Karakose. We are implementing and studying its core workflow as a student
prototype. We are not claiming to have reproduced every implementation detail
or created a system ready for patient care. We will explain what we have built,
what we have evaluated, how our work differs from the paper, and what remains.

SLIDE 2 - WHY THIS PROTOTYPE MATTERS
------------------------------------
MARK:
The motivation is that a fluent answer can still be unsupported. A system may
miss relevant medical evidence, and a single answer can be difficult to audit.
If that answer is weak, a single-pass system may not try again.

Our objective is to explore a workflow that structures a free-text case,
retrieves textbook and biomedical evidence, searches relevant PubMed literature,
compares the evidence, and revisits weak retrieval before presenting a traceable
result in a web interface.

The scope is research and education only. It is not a medical device, and it
must not be used to make decisions about a real patient's care.

SLIDE 3 - THE BASE PAPER
-----------------------
MARK:
The base paper describes five specialized roles, a retrieval and evidence
fusion workflow, and an optimizer that can request another analysis when the
result is weak. It reports BioBERT embeddings, Qdrant retrieval, external
literature, and a web interface that returns an annotated report.

The paper describes a confidence threshold of 0.65 and up to three
optimization cycles. Our goal is to recreate the main workflow, not to make a
bit-for-bit replication. As we compare the systems later, we will be explicit
about the models, data sources, orchestration, and scoring that we adapted.

SLIDE 4 - FROM INPUT TO RESULT
-----------------------------
MARK:
This diagram shows the main processing sequence. First, the Clarifier takes the
case text and extracts structured symptoms, duration, concepts, and a retrieval
query. It runs once before the iterative part.

Next, the RAG Analyzer embeds the query with the BioBERT-based model and searches
the local Qdrant collections. The Evidence Scanner searches PubMed for related
abstracts. Clinical Fusion compares the retrieved evidence, identifies support
or conflicts, and calculates our experimental heuristic score.

If the score meets the threshold, the pipeline prepares its final response and
evidence report. If the score is below threshold and cycles remain, the
Optimizer refines the retrieval focus and sends the case through retrieval,
PubMed search, and fusion again. The final response is generated after that
loop. This score is a research heuristic; it is not a probability that an
assessment is correct.

SLIDE 5 - WHAT THE FIVE ROLES DO
-------------------------------
MARK:
The five agents are five software roles in the pipeline. The Clarifier
structures the input. The RAG Analyzer retrieves passages and proposes
passage-linked candidate hypotheses. The Evidence Scanner searches PubMed.
Clinical Fusion compares the supporting and conflicting information. The
Adaptive Optimizer changes the query or focus when the score is low.

An important implementation detail is that we use one configured Gemini model
across these roles. Python code explicitly decides which role runs next and
passes each role its prompt and expected output. So these are separate agent
roles and separate model calls in an orchestrated workflow, but they are not
five different models or five independent services. That is one of our
adaptations from the paper.

SLIDE 6 - RETRIEVAL FOUNDATION
-----------------------------
MARK:
For retrieval, our local Qdrant index contains approximately 2,800 PubMed
abstracts, divided into about 11,244 chunks. It also contains 13,691 passages
sampled from 18 textbooks in the public MedRAG textbook dataset, with up to 780
passages sampled per book. Results from the two collections are combined, and
the default retrieval depth is five passages.

BioBERT converts the query and stored passages into numerical vectors so
Qdrant can find semantically similar text. BioBERT is the retriever's embedding
model; it does not generate the clinical response. The response is generated
by the language model using the pipeline's input and retrieved evidence.

One limitation is important: these PubMed abstracts are research literature,
not clinical guidelines. MedRAG is a research corpus, not an approved guideline
set. We are not claiming to use the exact unnamed textbook and guideline list
from the base paper.

SLIDE 7 - EVALUATION DESIGN
---------------------------
SAIMA:
We prepared fixed samples so that the three evaluation modes receive the same
questions. MedQA is an exam-style multiple-choice dataset, and we evaluated 20
questions from it. PubMedQA and MedBullets samples are prepared and frozen, but
we have not run those evaluations yet.

Here, freezing means fixing the sampled question IDs before comparing modes.
The seed of 42 makes that selection repeatable and allows us to audit cases.
It does not mean that the underlying datasets are ours or that the systems
were trained on these questions. Live free-text analysis is a separate
prototype demonstration, not part of the 20-question benchmark result.

SLIDE 9 - PRELIMINARY RESULTS
-----------------------------
SAIMA:
On the same 20 fixed MedQA questions, the baseline language model answered 17
correctly, or 85 percent. RAG-only also answered 17 correctly, or 85 percent.
The full pipeline answered 18 correctly, or 90 percent.

That is one additional correct answer for the full pipeline on this small
sample. It is an initial benchmark result, not proof that the full system is
statistically superior. We have not measured clinical outcomes or calibrated
the confidence score. We need more cases and further analysis before making
stronger claims.

SLIDE 10 - PAPER VERSUS OUR IMPLEMENTATION
-----------------------------------------
SAIMA:
This table summarizes the main adaptations. The paper describes multiple
models and CrewAI orchestration; our prototype uses one Gemini model with
explicit Python orchestration. The paper refers to textbooks, guidelines, and
literature; our retrieval uses a sampled MedRAG textbook collection and PubMed.

The paper includes terminology mapping, while our Clarifier structures fields
without SNOMED or ICD mapping. For external literature, we use PubMed
E-utilities rather than broader web intelligence. We use our own uncalibrated
heuristic score rather than claiming to reproduce a validated or exactly
matching paper score.

The shared core is the named workflow: BioBERT-based retrieval with Qdrant,
five functional roles, evidence fusion, and adaptive refinement. This is a
core-workflow recreation with documented differences, not an exact replication.

SLIDE 11 - LIMITATIONS AND NEXT WORK
-----------------------------------
SAIMA:
Our main limitations are the small evaluation set, one shared language model,
an uncalibrated score, no clinician review, and no clinical validation. We also
do not exactly recreate the paper's complete corpus or broader web search, and
live retrieval and model outputs can vary.

Our next steps are to run PubMedQA and MedBullets, expand MedQA, report
uncertainty, evaluate retrieval quality and grounding, review source quality
and score calibration, and add expert review or terminology mapping if
feasible. Our immediate priority is broader evaluation and careful error
analysis, not clinical deployment.

SLIDE 12 - CONCLUSION
--------------------
SAIMA:
To conclude, we have a working research prototype with a web interface and an
end-to-end workflow connecting case structuring, retrieval, literature search,
evidence comparison, and refinement. Our first MedQA comparison is 85 percent
for baseline, 85 percent for RAG-only, and 90 percent for the full pipeline.

These are preliminary results on 20 questions. The implementation differences
and limitations are part of the result, and we have stated them explicitly.
Our next step is to broaden and strengthen the evaluation. Thank you. We are
happy to answer questions.

   Do not claim five separate LLMs.
3. The 0.65 score is a heuristic threshold, not a calibrated probability or
   validated diagnostic confidence.
4. PubMedQA and MedBullets are frozen/prepared but not evaluated; do not quote
   results for them.
5. The 90% result is 18/20. Do not call it statistically significant or proof
