SLIDE 2 — PROBLEM, MOTIVATION & OBJECTIVES
------------------------------------------
Problem
  • A language model can answer medical questions fluently while missing
    relevant evidence, citing no sources, or failing to revisit weak results.
  • A single answer is difficult to audit: what evidence was retrieved, what
    disagreed, and why did the system stop?

Project objective
  Build and evaluate a prototype that:
  1. Structures a free-text clinical case.
  2. Retrieves biomedical and textbook passages.
  3. Checks related PubMed literature.
  4. Compares evidence and can refine retrieval when support is weak.
  5. Shows a traceable result through a web interface.

Scope statement
  Research and education prototype; not a medical device and not for patient
  care.

Speaker notes:
“Our goal is to reproduce the main workflow and study its behavior, not to
claim that it is ready to make clinical decisions.”


SLIDE 3 — BASE PAPER: WHAT THE AUTHORS PROPOSED
-----------------------------------------------
Base paper:
Öğdü, Ç. U., Arslanoğlu, K., & Karaköse, M. (2025). “An Adaptive Multi-Agent
LLM-Based Clinical Decision Support System Integrating Biomedical RAG and Web
Intelligence.” IEEE Access, 13, 167390–167404.
DOI: 10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3613340

The paper describes:
  • Five specialized roles arranged as a sequential workflow with feedback.
  • Biomedical semantic retrieval using BioBERT embeddings and Qdrant.
  • External web/literature evidence to complement the local corpus.
  • Evidence fusion and an adaptive optimizer for low-confidence cases.
  • A web prototype with free-text clinical input and an annotated report.
  • A confidence threshold of 0.65 and up to three optimization cycles.

Interpretation for our project:
  We reproduce the workflow’s main ideas. We do not claim a bit-for-bit
  replication of every model, corpus, search service, or orchestration detail.

Speaker notes:
“The paper is our architectural reference. Where its exact implementation or
corpus details were unavailable or impractical, we chose an explicit and
documented approximation.”


SLIDE 4 — OUR WORKFLOW: FROM INPUT TO RESULT
--------------------------------------------
  Free-text case / saved benchmark question
                    ↓
  1. Clarifier: symptoms, duration, concepts, missing details, query
                    ↓
  2. RAG Analyzer: embed query → search local Qdrant → candidate hypotheses
                    ↓
  3. Evidence Scanner: search PubMed for relevant abstracts
                    ↓
  4. Fusion: compare sources → summarize support/conflicts → score heuristic
                    ↓
  Score ≥ 0.65? ─ Yes → final response and evidence report
       │
       No, cycles remain
       ↓
  5. Optimizer: refine retrieval focus → repeat retrieval/evidence/fusion

Important detail:
  Clarifier runs before the loop. The retrieval, evidence search, and fusion
  run once per cycle. Final response generation happens after the loop.

Speaker notes:
“Python controls the sequence and loop. The user does not need to operate the
agents separately.”


SLIDE 5 — THE FIVE AGENT ROLES IN OUR IMPLEMENTATION
----------------------------------------------------
Role                 Responsibility in this project
-------------------  -----------------------------------------------
Clinical Clarifier   Converts input into structured case fields and query.
RAG Analyzer         Retrieves passages and proposes passage-linked candidates.
Evidence Scanner     Searches PubMed for abstracts related to the case/candidates.
Clinical Fusion      Compares local and PubMed evidence; reports gaps/conflicts.
Adaptive Optimizer   If score is low, proposes a refined query/focus for another cycle.

What “agent” means here:
  • Each role has its own prompt, input/output contract, and step in the flow.
  • The same configured Gemini model is called separately for LLM tasks.
  • Python orchestrates the calls; these are not five independent models or
    five autonomous services.
  • Embedding, vector search, PubMed retrieval, scoring, logging, and display
    are performed by separate software components.

Speaker notes:
“We use five specialized roles with one underlying LLM. We are transparent
that this is less model-diverse than the paper, which reports multiple models.”


SLIDE 6 — DATA, CORPUS & BIOBERT
-------------------------------
Local retrieval index (Qdrant, embedded/local mode)
  • Biomedical collection: about 2,800 PubMed abstracts; about 11,244 chunks.
  • Textbooks collection: sampled passages from 18 books in the public MedRAG
    textbook corpus; up to 780 passages per book; 13,691 indexed passages.
  • Retrieval combines results across collections; top-k is 5 by default.

Embedding model
  `pritamdeka/BioBERT-mnli-snli-scinli-scitail-mednli-stsb`
  • Encodes the query and passages into vectors for semantic similarity search.
  • It retrieves relevant text; it does not generate the clinical answer.
  • The project README records this model ID as matching the one named in the
    base paper.

Source distinction
  • PubMed is an official NLM biomedical citation/abstract resource.
  • MedRAG textbooks are a public research corpus, not an approved guideline set.
  • Our corpus and live search sources are not identical to the paper’s full
    corpus and broader web-intelligence setup.

Speaker notes:
“The paper does not provide a book list we could reproduce. We therefore used
a public MedRAG textbook corpus and documented the sampling. PubMed abstracts
are not the same thing as clinical guidelines.”


SLIDE 7 — BENCHMARKS: WHAT WE FROZE AND WHY
-------------------------------------------
Dataset       Purpose / format                         Project status
------------  ---------------------------------------  -------------------------
MedQA         US medical exam-style MCQ                Evaluated: 20 questions
PubMedQA      Research questions from PubMed abstracts Prepared/frozen; not run
MedBullets    Medical exam questions                  Prepared/frozen; not run

Why freeze samples?
  • Same question IDs across baseline, RAG-only, and full-pipeline runs.
  • Seed 42 makes the sampled set repeatable.
  • Enables a fairer within-project comparison and audit of cases.
  • “Frozen” refers to evaluation items; live free-text analysis remains open.

Scope limitation:
  The paper reports broader dataset evaluation. Our reported result is only the
  20-question MedQA sample; do not imply the other datasets were evaluated.

Speaker notes:
“The saved demo answer choices are shown because those records are exam
questions. A live user does not need to select a benchmark or provide options.”


SLIDE 8 — PROTOTYPE & DEMONSTRATION
-----------------------------------
The web page demonstrates:
  • Free-text input and an Analyze action.
  • Structured summary: extracted symptoms, duration, and search concepts.
  • Iteration trace: passages, hypotheses, PubMed records, and stop reason.
  • Final generated response, experimental score, and traceable sources.

Saved demonstrations
  • `medqa_347`: normal workflow; one iteration meets threshold.
  • `medqa_107`: adaptive workflow; first cycle is low, optimizer refines the
    query, second cycle meets threshold.

Live vs. replay
  • Live free-text analysis calls the configured Gemini API and PubMed, then
    writes a local audit log.
  • Saved demonstrations replay existing logs and do not make API calls.

Speaker notes:
“A RAG candidate is not a confirmed diagnosis. In the live fever/nosebleed
example, the candidate phrase describes a symptom/evidence topic; the generated
response recommends evaluation and does not establish the cause.”


SLIDE 9 — EARLY EVALUATION RESULTS
----------------------------------
Configuration       Correct / valid    Accuracy       Status
------------------  -----------------  -------------  ------------------------
Baseline LLM        17 / 20            85.0%          Complete
RAG-only            17 / 20            85.0%          Complete
Full pipeline       18 / 20            90.0%          Complete

Evaluation setup
  • 20 fixed MedQA questions; same sampled IDs for the three modes.
  • Baseline: LLM without retrieval.
  • RAG-only: local retrieval plus answer selection; no fusion/feedback loop.
  • Full: Clarifier, RAG, PubMed, fusion, optimizer loop, final selection.

Interpretation
  • Full pipeline scored one question higher than baseline/RAG-only in this
    small sample.
  • This is a preliminary result, not evidence of statistical superiority.
  • No clinical outcome or confidence-calibration evaluation has been done.

Speaker notes:
“The result supports continued investigation, but a 20-question sample is too
small to make a strong general claim. We will report counts, not just percentages.”

SLIDE 11 — LIMITATIONS & PLAN TO COMPLETE THE PROJECT
-----------------------------------------------------
Current limitations
  • Only 20 MedQA questions evaluated; PubMedQA and MedBullets not yet run.
  • One underlying Gemini model across roles; roles are prompt-separated, not
    independent models.
  • Confidence is an uncalibrated heuristic, not a probability.
  • No clinical validation, clinician review, or patient-safety study.
  • No SNOMED CT/ICD-10 coding; no exact recreation of the paper’s corpus or
    wider web search; PubMed publication-type levels are simple rules.
  • Live retrieval and LLM output can vary; external service availability affects
    runtime.

Next steps (prioritized)
  1. Run and report MedBullets and PubMedQA with suitable dataset-specific
     scoring; preserve failures and avoid unsupported paper comparisons.
  2. Expand the MedQA sample and report per-case results plus uncertainty.
  3. Evaluate retrieval quality and answer grounding separately from accuracy.
  4. Review evidence classification, source quality, and confidence calibration.
  5. Add clinician/expert review and structured terminology mapping if feasible.
  6. Compare role-specific models and orchestration alternatives only if time
     and resources allow.

Speaker notes:
“Our immediate completion work is evaluation and careful error analysis. Any
clinical validation or deployment would require a separate, much more rigorous
program.”


SLIDE 12 — CONCLUSION & REFERENCES
---------------------------------
Progress summary
  • An end-to-end local web prototype now supports both free-text analysis and
    replay of saved benchmark cases.
  • The five-role workflow links case structuring, biomedical retrieval,
    PubMed evidence, fusion, and adaptive re-querying.
  • The first 20-question MedQA comparison is complete: 85%, 85%, and 90% for
    baseline, RAG-only, and full pipeline respectively.
